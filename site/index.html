Questions

Want the numbers for plain roasted chana? Read our breakdown.

Can I buy DumDana right now?

Not yet. DumDana is a market test run by a college team. We're checking whether people want a ₹10 protein namkeen before making it. Join the waitlist and you'll hear first if it goes ahead.

Where do the protein numbers come from?

We calculated them from published nutrition data: Indian food composition tables for roasted chana, and USDA data for soy nuts and peanuts. They're targets, not lab results. No value goes on a real pack until an accredited lab has tested it.

Why 25 g in the ₹10 pack and not more?

After the shop's margin, the distributor's margin and 5% GST, a ₹10 pack leaves the maker about ₹6.50. A 30 g pack would leave almost nothing for packaging and transport. 25 g keeps the price honest. If you want the most protein for your money, the ₹20 pack (60 g, about 15.6 g of protein) is the better deal.

Is it fried?

No. The chana, soy nuts and peanuts are dry-roasted. A little oil is used only to make the masala stick.

What are the allergens?

DumDana contains peanuts and soy. Please skip it if you're allergic to either.

Will it help me build muscle or lose weight?

We don't make health claims. DumDana is a namkeen with a lot more protein per rupee than most snacks. How it fits into your diet is up to you and, if you need advice, a qualified nutritionist.

How is this different from protein chips?

Protein chips cost around ₹50 for 10 g of protein. DumDana gets its protein from whole roasted chana, soy and peanuts, and its ₹20 pack works out to about ₹12.80 for the same 10 g.

When will DumDana launch?

There's no date yet. If the market test shows enough people want it, the next step is a small sample batch and a lab test. Waitlist members hear first, and we'll tell you honestly if we decide not to go ahead.
